PROJECT OVERVIEW

Understanding workforce attrition

This project analyses workforce and employee attrition data across Nigeria and Kenya from 2019 to 2025.

The analysis examines where employee turnover is highest, workforce patterns, and recruitment trends, while addressing data quality issues that could affect the reliability of the findings. It highlights areas where management may need to look closer.

Dataset

2,500 employees across Nigeria and Kenya.

Time Period

2019–2025.

Data Sources

9 core HR datasets covering workforce, recruitment, performance, and employee records, supported by 12 lookup tables used to standardize, validate, and connect the data before analysis.

Tools

Excel, Power Query, Power BI and DAX.

ANALYSIS QUESTIONS

What did the analysis try to answer?

Where is employee attrition highest?
Which departments require attention?
Which locations have the highest attrition?
Which recruitment sources generate hires?
DATA PREPARATION

From raw data to analysis-ready data

The project involved reviewing nine core HR datasets, supported by 12 lookup tables, and preparing the data for analysis.

The data contained issues such as duplicate records, missing values and inconsistent formatting. The datasets were cleaned and structured before relationships were created for analysis.

RECOMMENDATIONS

What could management investigate next?

01 — Investigate attrition hotspots

Review the drivers behind higher attrition in locations and departments with elevated employee exits.

02 — Examine early-tenure exits

Investigate the employee experience during the first few years, where a notable share of exits occurred.

03 — Review recruitment channels

Compare recruitment sources and their contribution to successful hires to support future recruitment planning.
